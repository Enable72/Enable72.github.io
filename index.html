About Me

Hi, I'm Atishna Samantaray, a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of California, San Diego, advised by Prof. Pamela Cosman. My research focuses on generative and diffusion models, multimodal video synthesis, and cross-view representation learning, with applications in perceptual image/video compression, 3D perception on autonomous-driving sensor data, and medical imaging.

I like designing and validating models end to end: from architecture and training to rigorous human and model evaluation. The ideas behind my projects are my own. I think a problem through from first principles, and I shape what I find into concrete, testable designs; that way of thinking is what I consider my greatest strength. Currently, I am working on reconstructing egocentric video from compact multimodal signals for AR/VR skill-transfer applications.

I'm always happy to talk about research and collaborations. Feel free to reach out by email!

Attention Where It Matters: Implicit Saliency Priors in Learned Image Compression

Submitted to IEEE TIP

What if a compression model learned where people actually look? Instead of spending bits evenly across the whole image, it would invest them in the regions humans notice, while keeping everything else clean enough not to pull the eye away.

Our answer is Saliency-Driven Attention (SDA): human gaze maps act as a soft prior that gently nudges a transformer's attention during training, rather than a hard rule about where bits must go. At test time it needs no gaze input and adds no deployment cost.

  • Up to 15–20% better LPIPS and DISTS across three codecs (STF, TCM, SwinT) on Kodak, CLIC2020, and Tecnick-40.
  • People noticed. In a 30-participant study (216 image pairs each, matched bitrates), SDA reconstructions were preferred in 50.6% of trials vs. 31.6% for baselines (p < 0.001), and 1.9x more often than implicit-attention baselines at medium-to-high compression.

Cross-View Attention and Diffusion Synthesis for Maxillary Sinus Pathology Assessment

Submitted to IEEE TMI · CBCT medical imaging

Radiologists rarely diagnose a sinus from a single plane. Why should an AI? They read coronal and sagittal views together, yet most models look at only one, and key findings like odontogenic sinusitis are rare in the data.

We tackled both problems at once, using CBCT scans from 194 patients: a bidirectional cross-view attention model that lets the two views exchange information before predicting, and a structure-guided latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion + LoRA) that draws realistic rare pathologies from anatomical masks to rebalance the training data.

  • Two views beat one: up to +10.8% classification accuracy over the coronal-only baseline and +4.1% over sagittal-only (CNN+ViT), with similar gains for YOLOv5 and ResNet18. Segmentation Dice rises from 0.712 to 0.829 (coronal) and from 0.745 to 0.845 (sagittal).
  • The attention is the point: swapping it for simple feature concatenation costs 2.8 points of accuracy and 3.3 points of macro F1.
  • Synthetic scans a radiologist believed: 887 generated images, judged anatomically plausible by a board-certified oral and maxillofacial radiologist, with no significant distribution gap from real scans (KID, p = 0.881). Augmenting with them helps most for the rarest pathologies.
  • Stays best on an independent external dataset without fine-tuning, with about 7–20% accuracy improvements across settings.

Can We Remove the Ground? Obstacle-aware Point Cloud Compression for Remote Object Detection

ICNC 2026 · LiDAR · 3D perception

A 64-line LiDAR streams about 128 Mbps, more than today's vehicle-to-infrastructure links can carry. So can we simply stop sending the ground? It turns out 3D detectors lean on the ground more than you would expect, especially the points right below and around objects: removing all of it cost over 3% detection accuracy for cars.

So we kept the ground that matters. Pillar-based Ground Removal (PGR) is a lightweight, highly parallelizable algorithm that removes pillars that are likely ground, then restores the ones near obstacles, with no object detector needed.

  • State-of-the-art detectors (SECOND, PVT-SSD) work equally well with 20–30% of points removed on KITTI and Waymo Open Dataset, about 10% in dense urban frames and up to 50% on open roads.
  • On Waymo, PGR saves 9.34% to 12.94% of the bit rate with mAP changes of at most 0.21%, and it beats other state-of-the-art ground removal methods on every tested object class.
  • Runs at 86.5 FPS (11.6 ms per frame) on a single RTX 3090, leaving ample latency budget for detection.
